My research is in behavioral and experimental economics, cultural economics and political economy. A first line of my work studies the economic relevance of cognitive uncertainty, showing how its measurement brings together many behavioral economics anomalies in consumer decision making, and simultaneously facilitates insights about which anomalies and biases affect market outcomes. A second line of work studies the political economy consequences of heterogeneity in moral values. Finally, a third line of work consists of large-scale multinational surveys that measure the distribution of economic and moral preferences across cultures. Methodologically, my work relies on a combination of experiments, multinational surveys, formal theory, and machine learning techniques to extract complexity features.
